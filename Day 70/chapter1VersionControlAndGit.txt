The Three Stages
1. The Working Directory (The Sandbox)
What it is: The actual folder on your computer where you are creating, editing, and deleting files.

The State: Git sees these files, but they are "untracked" or "modified." If your computer crashes right now, Git can't help you recover these changes.

2. The Staging Area (The Loading Dock)
What it is: A middle-ground draft area (often called the "Index").

The State: You’re telling Git, "Hey, I want these specific changes to be in my next snapshot, but I'm not quite ready to lock them in yet." It lets you curate exactly what goes into your history.

3. The Git Repository (The Vault)
What it is: The .git folder hidden in your project directory.

The State: Once you commit, your files are permanently compressed and saved into Git's history with a unique ID (SHA-1 hash). They are safe here.
